description: Generate a full character profile for an anti-hero, rival or arch-nemesis. Use when asked to design, create, invent or flesh out an anti-hero, villain, rival, nemesis or foil for a character, mascot, story, game or brand, including backstory, flaws, gadgets, rivalry and villain quotes.
---

You build a complete, usable anti-hero profile that is funny, specific, and tied to the hero it opposes.

## Inputs

- **Hero:** the character this one opposes (name, what they stand for). If none is given, ask once; if the user has no hero, create a short one-line hero first and say so.
- **Tone:** comedic (default), dark-comedy, or mostly serious. Keep it all-ages unless the user asks otherwise.
- **Setting and genre:** infer from the hero if not given (cartoon, sci-fi, fantasy, mascot, holiday, workplace).
- **Role:** anti-hero (flawed but sympathetic), rival (competitive peer), or arch-nemesis (true opposition). Default: rival with villain energy.
- **Constraints:** name length, existing lore to respect, audience age.

If the project has existing lore, mascots or brand voice, read it first and keep the new character consistent with it.

## Profile: fill every section

1. **Name and title:** a memorable name plus an overblown self-given title ("Baron Von Snowdrift, Destroyer of Lunch Breaks").
2. **One-line pitch:** what they want and why the hero is in the way.
3. **Look:** three distinctive visual details (silhouette, signature item, one thing that is always slightly wrong).
4. **Backstory:** a short origin in 4-6 sentences. It must include a **wound** (the real reason they became this way) and an **inciting grievance** against the hero.
5. **Motivation:** the stated goal versus the real need underneath it. The gap between them drives the comedy and the sympathy.
6. **Personality and flaws:** 3-4 traits. At least two flaws must be self-sabotaging (they cause the character's own defeats), and one must be a flaw the hero also secretly shares.
7. **Skills and weaknesses:** what they are genuinely good at, and the specific thing that reliably beats them.
8. **Comedic gadgets:** 3-5 inventions or tools. Each has a name, what it is meant to do, and how it fails. Make the failure follow from the character's flaws, not random.
9. **Rival dynamic:** how they and the hero relate: what each respects about the other, a running gag in their fights, and what each would lose without the other. Give one scene of them in conflict, 3-5 lines.
10. **Villainous quotes:** 6-8 original lines: a monologue opener, a threat, a gloat, a defeat line, a rare moment of honesty, and a catchphrase. Vary the rhythm; keep each under 25 words.
11. **Story hooks:** 3 ready-to-use plot ideas that put this character in motion.

## Quality bar

- Specific beats generic: a named gadget with a particular failure is better than "an evil machine".
- The humor comes from the character's logic taken too seriously, not from random absurdity.
- The rival must feel like a real mirror of the hero, so each is defined by the other.
- Make the sympathetic wound real. Even a joke villain needs a reason the audience half-understands.
- Avoid stock traits (tragic dead parent, "I want to rule the world") unless you give them a fresh twist.

## Output format

Use a markdown profile with a heading per section above, in that order. Keep the whole profile to about 600-900 words. End with one line offering variations: a darker version, a nicer version, or a second rival.

## Rules

- Original characters only: do not copy or closely imitate existing copyrighted characters, and do not use real people.
- Keep content free of slurs, real-world hate, and real tragedies played for laughs. Villainy stays fictional and comedic or story-driven.
- Do not invent facts about the user's real brand, products or staff; if the villain is for a brand, tie it only to details the user gave.
- Do not change existing canon: if lore exists, add to it and flag anything that conflicts.
- Save to a file only if asked; default is to answer in chat.
